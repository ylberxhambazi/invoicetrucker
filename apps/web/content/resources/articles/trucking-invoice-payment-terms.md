# Trucking Invoice Payment Terms: Net 15, Net 30, Net 45, QuickPay, and Factoring

Getting a load delivered does not mean the money arrives immediately.

For owner-operators and small fleets, the time between **delivery** and **payment** can make a major difference to cash flow. Fuel, insurance, maintenance, payroll, tolls, and repairs still need to be paid while invoices are waiting.

That is why trucking invoice payment terms matter.

The most common arrangements include:

- Net 15
- Net 30
- Net 45
- QuickPay
- Factoring

The right option depends on the broker or shipper, your contract, your cash-flow needs, and the fees you are willing to accept.

## What are trucking invoice payment terms?

Payment terms define when an invoice is expected to be paid.

If an invoice says **Net 30**, that usually means payment is due within 30 days of the agreed starting point, often the invoice date or the date the payer considers the invoice complete and accepted.

That distinction matters.

A broker may not start the payment clock until it has received:

- The invoice
- Signed POD or BOL
- Rate confirmation
- Required accessorial receipts
- Any other supporting paperwork

So the real workflow is often:

**Delivery → Documents complete → Invoice submitted → Payment term begins → Paid**

## Net 15 vs Net 30 vs Net 45

| Payment Term | Typical Meaning | Cash-Flow Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net 15 | Payment due in about 15 days | Faster |
| Net 30 | Payment due in about 30 days | Common middle ground |
| Net 45 | Payment due in about 45 days | Slower |
| QuickPay | Faster payment for a fee | Fastest without factoring |
| Factoring | Factor advances cash against invoice | Very fast, with factoring cost |

The exact payment date still depends on the customer’s billing rules, weekends, holidays, invoice acceptance, and document completeness.

## What does Net 30 mean in trucking?

Net 30 generally means the payer expects to pay the invoice within 30 days under the agreed billing terms.

Example:

- Load delivered: October 1
- Invoice submitted and accepted: October 2
- Payment term: Net 30
- Expected due date: around November 1

But if the invoice is missing a required POD and is not accepted until October 7, the practical payment timeline may start later.

This is why a clean billing package matters.

## Is Net 30 better than Net 45?

From a carrier cash-flow perspective, Net 30 is usually better than Net 45 because you receive cash sooner.

But the practical answer depends on:

- The customer’s reliability
- The freight rate
- Whether QuickPay is available
- Whether you use factoring
- Your operating cash reserve
- How many loads you carry at once

A reliable Net 45 customer can still be better than a Net 15 customer that constantly disputes invoices.

The goal is not only faster payment. The goal is **predictable payment**.

## What is QuickPay in trucking?

QuickPay is a broker or platform option that pays the carrier sooner than standard terms, usually in exchange for a fee.

A standard payment arrangement might be Net 30, while QuickPay may offer payment in a few business days.

QuickPay can be useful when:

- Fuel expenses are high
- You need working capital quickly
- You do not want a long-term factoring relationship
- The broker’s QuickPay fee is acceptable

Before using it, check:

- Fee percentage
- Payment timing
- Whether every load qualifies
- Whether enrollment is required
- Whether the fee changes by payment speed

QuickPay is not free cash. It is a trade-off between speed and margin.

## What is freight factoring?

Freight factoring is different from QuickPay.

With factoring, a carrier sells or assigns eligible invoices to a factoring company. The factor advances most of the invoice value and then collects payment from the broker or shipper.

The exact structure varies by provider.

Factoring can help with:

- Fuel
- Repairs
- Payroll
- Insurance
- Working capital
- Growth

But it also adds:

- Fees
- Contract terms
- Documentation requirements
- Possible recourse obligations
- Notice-of-assignment requirements

If you use factoring, your invoice may need remittance instructions telling the payer where payment should be sent.

## QuickPay vs factoring

| QuickPay | Factoring |
| --- | --- |
| Offered by the broker/platform | Offered by a factoring company |
| Usually load-specific | Often ongoing relationship |
| Fee deducted by broker/platform | Factoring fee charged by factor |
| Payer stays within broker process | Payment may be assigned to factor |
| Good for occasional fast payment | Good for continuous cash-flow support |

Neither is automatically better.

A carrier that needs occasional fast cash may prefer QuickPay. A fleet that constantly carries a large receivables balance may find factoring more practical.

## What payment terms should appear on a trucking invoice?

A clean invoice should make the payment expectation obvious.

Include:

- Invoice date
- Payment term
- Due date if known
- Remittance details
- Invoice number
- Load number
- Total due

Example:

> **Invoice Date:** October 15, 2026  
> **Payment Terms:** Net 30  
> **Due Date:** November 14, 2026

If the contract controls the due date, your invoice should match the agreed terms.

Do not put Net 15 on the invoice if the broker-carrier agreement says Net 30 and expect the shorter term to override the agreement automatically.

## When does the payment clock start?

Possible starting points include:

- Invoice date
- Invoice receipt date
- Invoice approval date
- Delivery date
- Date complete supporting paperwork is received

Your customer’s agreement and billing instructions control this.

That is why you should know exactly what the customer considers a **complete invoice package**.

## What can delay payment?

Common causes include:

- Missing POD
- Missing BOL
- Wrong load number
- Wrong invoice amount
- Missing rate confirmation
- Unsupported detention or lumper charge
- Incorrect remittance instructions
- Duplicate invoice number
- Invoice sent to the wrong email or portal
- Customer dispute

A payment term can say Net 30, but a bad invoice can turn it into Net 45, Net 60, or worse.

## How should owner-operators choose payment terms?

Owner-operators usually do not have complete freedom to choose. The broker or shipper often defines the standard payment term.

Still, you can evaluate the full deal.

Ask:

- What is the standard payment term?
- Is QuickPay available?
- What is the QuickPay fee?
- Is factoring allowed?
- What documents are required?
- When does the payment term officially start?
- How are disputes handled?
- Is there a billing portal?

A $3,000 load at Net 45 can affect your business differently than the same load at Net 15.

## Should you offer customers Net 30?

If you bill direct shippers, you may have more control over payment terms.

Net 30 is common in B2B billing because it gives the customer time to process invoices while still providing a defined due date.

Before offering longer terms, consider:

- Customer creditworthiness
- Your cash reserve
- Average invoice size
- Monthly load volume
- Your own supplier/payment obligations

Longer terms effectively mean you are financing the customer for longer.

## How to calculate a due date

If the agreed term is Net 30:

**Due Date = Payment-Term Start Date + 30 days**

Example:

- Invoice accepted: October 10
- Net 30
- Due: November 9

Be careful when the agreement specifies business days instead of calendar days.

## What if an invoice becomes overdue?

Do not wait until it is 30 days late before noticing.

A simple process is:

- Before due date: no action unless documents are missing
- Due date: confirm payment status
- 3–5 days overdue: polite reminder
- 7–14 days overdue: stronger follow-up
- Longer delay: escalate according to your contract and business process

Keep all follow-up documented.

A useful invoice status flow is:

**Sent → Due → Overdue → Follow-up → Paid**

## Track due dates, not just invoice dates

A list of invoices is not enough.

You should be able to answer:

- Which invoices are due this week?
- Which invoices are overdue?
- Which customers pay slowly?
- How much cash is outstanding?
- Which invoices are on QuickPay?
- Which invoices were factored?

That is where a dedicated invoice workflow becomes more useful than a folder full of PDFs.

## Payment terms and invoice aging

Invoice aging groups unpaid invoices by how long they have been outstanding.

Typical aging buckets are:

- Current
- 1–30 days overdue
- 31–60 days overdue
- 61–90 days overdue
- 90+ days overdue

This helps you see whether unpaid invoices are becoming a cash-flow problem.

## Common mistakes with trucking payment terms

### Assuming Net 30 starts on delivery

The customer may define the start differently.

### Forgetting QuickPay fees

Faster payment can reduce the amount you receive.

### Sending incomplete paperwork

The invoice may not enter the payment queue at all.

### Using the wrong due date

The invoice should match the agreed payment terms.

### Not tracking overdue invoices

A forgotten invoice is easy to miss when you manage billing through email and spreadsheets.

### Ignoring factoring remittance instructions

If a factor owns the receivable, payment instructions must be accurate.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does Net 30 mean for truck drivers and owner-operators?

It generally means the invoice is due within 30 days under the agreed billing terms.

### Is QuickPay worth it?

It can be when cash-flow speed is more valuable than the fee. Compare the fee against your actual need for faster payment.

### Is factoring the same as QuickPay?

No. QuickPay is usually offered by the broker or platform. Factoring involves a separate factoring company purchasing or financing the receivable.

### Can I change payment terms on my invoice?

You can display terms, but your invoice does not automatically override the underlying agreement with the customer.

### Should I include a due date?

Yes, when you can determine it accurately from the agreed terms.

## From completed load to collected cash

For a small trucking business, the billing process does not end when the invoice is sent.

The complete workflow is:

**Load → Delivery → Documents → Invoice → Due Date → Payment**

Knowing the payment terms helps you understand when cash should arrive and when follow-up is needed.
