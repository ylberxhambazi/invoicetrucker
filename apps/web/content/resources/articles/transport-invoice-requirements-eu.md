# Transport Invoice Requirements in the EU

A transport invoice needs to do more than show a total amount.

For a road freight or logistics business operating in the European Union, the invoice should clearly identify:

- Who supplied the service
- Who bought the service
- What transport service was supplied
- When it was supplied
- How much is payable
- How VAT is treated
- Which additional wording is required for the transaction

EU VAT rules provide a common invoicing baseline, but Member States can also apply national requirements in certain areas.

That means there is no safe rule that says:

> “If my invoice works in one EU country, it automatically satisfies every requirement everywhere.”

The right approach is to use the EU baseline as the foundation and then check the rules that apply to the supplier, customer and transaction.

> **Important:** This guide covers the EU VAT invoicing baseline for transport businesses. It is general information, not legal or tax advice.

## What is a transport invoice?

A transport invoice is the commercial billing document issued for a freight or transport service.

For example, a carrier may invoice for:

- Road freight
- Line haul
- Fuel surcharge
- Additional stops
- Waiting or detention
- Loading/unloading charges
- Other agreed accessorials

An invoice is different from operational transport documents such as:

- CMR consignment note
- Proof of delivery
- Delivery note
- Weight ticket
- Customs paperwork

Those documents may support the invoice, but they do not replace the invoice.

## When is an invoice required under EU VAT rules?

Under the EU VAT invoicing framework, invoices are generally required for most B2B supplies made by VAT-registered businesses.

There are exceptions and Member State-specific rules, but for ordinary B2B transport services the invoice is a central part of the VAT record.

The European Commission notes that EU businesses operate under a common set of basic VAT invoicing rules while individual Member States can have specific national provisions.

## Core information on a full EU VAT invoice

Depending on the transaction, a full VAT invoice generally needs information covering the following areas.

### 1. Invoice date

The invoice should show the date it was issued.

Example:

**Invoice date: 15 November 2026**

### 2. Unique sequential invoice number

The invoice needs a unique number that identifies it within the invoice sequence.

Examples:

- INV-2026-1048
- 2026/1048

The numbering approach should be controlled and consistent with the business's accounting requirements.

### 3. Supplier details

Include the supplier's:

- Full legal name
- Address
- VAT identification number where required

Do not rely only on a trading name if the legal invoicing entity is different.

### 4. Customer details

Include the customer's:

- Full legal name
- Address
- VAT identification number where required by the transaction

For cross-border B2B services, identifying the correct contractual customer is particularly important because customer status and establishment can affect the VAT treatment.

### 5. Description of the transport service

The invoice should make the service understandable.

Instead of:

**Service — €2,000**

use something clearer, for example:

**Road freight transport — Vienna, AT to Munich, DE — Load 48291**

Other useful references can include:

- Load number
- Customer reference
- Route
- Vehicle/trailer reference where relevant
- Pickup/delivery dates

Do not overload the invoice with operational information that belongs in the transport document, but provide enough detail to identify what is being billed.

### 6. Date of supply or completion

Where required, include the date on which the service was supplied or completed if it differs from the invoice date.

For transport this may correspond to the relevant service completion/delivery date, depending on the billing and tax rules that apply.

### 7. Quantity, unit or pricing basis where relevant

A freight invoice may be a flat-rate transport service, but where pricing depends on quantities or units, the invoice should make the calculation understandable.

Examples:

- 1 freight movement × €1,800
- 2.5 hours detention × €60
- 3 additional stops × €50

### 8. Net amount

Show the amount before VAT.

Example:

**Net amount: €2,000.00**

### 9. VAT rate and VAT amount where VAT is charged

Where the supplier charges VAT, the invoice should show the applicable VAT information required for that transaction.

Do not assume that the rate used in the supplier's home country automatically applies to every international transport service.

### 10. Total amount payable

The invoice should make the final amount due clear.

Example:

| Description | Net |
| --- | ---: |
| Road freight | €1,800 |
| Fuel surcharge | €200 |
| Net total | €2,000 |
| VAT | As applicable |
| **Total due** | **Final invoice amount** |

## Additional wording when reverse charge applies

When the customer is liable for the VAT under the reverse-charge procedure, EU VAT invoicing rules require the words:

**“reverse charge”**

on the invoice.

For example, a business may use wording such as:

> Reverse charge — VAT payable by the customer.

Some businesses also reference the relevant provision of the VAT Directive.

The actual VAT treatment should be confirmed before the invoice is issued.

For a more detailed explanation, see:

[VAT on Freight Transport Invoices in the EU](/resources/vat-on-freight-transport-invoices-eu)

## Other cases can require additional invoice wording

EU VAT invoicing rules identify additional wording for specific cases.

Examples include:

- Reverse charge
- Self-billing
- Cash accounting
- Certain exempt supplies
- Margin schemes
- Tax representative details where relevant

A normal road freight invoice will not use all of these.

The point is that the required text depends on the transaction.

## EU baseline does not eliminate national rules

The EU VAT Directive provides a shared framework, but individual Member States can have national invoicing rules.

Transport businesses should therefore verify:

- Country-specific invoice requirements
- Language requirements where applicable
- Domestic VAT rules
- Invoice retention obligations
- E-invoicing requirements
- Structured invoice requirements
- Real-time or digital reporting obligations

This is becoming increasingly important as Member States introduce different domestic e-invoicing systems and timelines.

A compliant PDF invoice in one context may not satisfy a structured e-invoicing mandate in another.

## “EU” and “Europe” are not the same thing

This distinction matters.

The EU VAT framework discussed here applies to European Union Member States.

Countries such as:

- United Kingdom
- Switzerland
- Norway
- Serbia
- Turkey
- North Macedonia

are European countries but are not EU Member States.

They have their own VAT, invoicing or indirect-tax rules.

That is why this resource is titled:

**Transport Invoice Requirements in the EU**

not “Europe.”

## What is a CMR consignment note?

The CMR consignment note is one of the most important documents in international European road freight.

The CMR Convention applies to contracts for the international carriage of goods by road for reward when the conditions of the Convention are met — notably where the places of taking over and delivery are in different countries and at least one is a contracting country.

The consignment note documents the contract of carriage and contains transport information.

Under the Convention, it includes information such as:

- Sender
- Carrier
- Place/date of taking over the goods
- Delivery location
- Consignee
- Description of the goods
- Number of packages
- Gross weight or other quantity
- Carriage-related charges
- Customs/formality instructions
- Statement that the carriage is subject to the CMR Convention

## Is the CMR consignment note an invoice?

No.

This distinction is important.

**CMR consignment note**
→ Transport/contract document

**Invoice**
→ Commercial and VAT billing document

The CMR note can support an invoice by showing what was transported and the parties involved, but it does not replace the supplier's VAT invoice.

A transport company may therefore keep:

**Rate/transport agreement → CMR → delivery evidence → invoice → payment**

as parts of the same commercial record.

## CMR and invoice information should agree

Although they serve different purposes, major references should not contradict each other.

Check that information such as:

- Customer
- Route
- Shipment/load reference
- Relevant dates
- Service description

is consistent across the transport paperwork and invoice.

Mismatch does not automatically mean the invoice is legally invalid, but it can cause:

- Customer questions
- Payment delays
- Accounting problems
- Audit/reconciliation difficulties

## What about e-CMR?

The CMR framework also supports electronic consignment notes through the e-CMR system for participating countries.

An electronic transport document is still conceptually separate from the invoice.

Do not confuse:

**electronic CMR**

with:

**electronic VAT invoice**

or:

**structured e-invoice**

They solve different problems and can be subject to different technical and legal requirements.

## PDF invoice vs structured e-invoice

A PDF is a visual electronic document.

A structured e-invoice contains machine-readable invoice data in a required structured format.

Those are not always legally interchangeable.

Several EU countries now have or are introducing B2B structured e-invoicing requirements.

Before operating in a market with a national mandate, check whether:

- PDF is accepted
- A structured format is mandatory
- A national platform is mandatory
- Peppol or another network is required
- Reporting must happen in real time or near real time

## What transport businesses should keep with an invoice

Depending on the customer and transaction, retain supporting records such as:

- CMR consignment note
- Proof of delivery
- Transport order
- Rate agreement
- Customer purchase order
- Detention/waiting approval
- Loading/unloading receipt
- Customs documentation
- Other agreed accessorial evidence

Not every invoice needs every document.

The customer's commercial requirements and the applicable legal/tax obligations determine what is necessary.

A practical invoice might look like:

### Supplier

**Example Transport GmbH**  
Supplier address  
VAT ID

### Customer

**Example Logistics B.V.**  
Customer address  
VAT ID

### Invoice

**Invoice number:** INV-2026-1048  
**Invoice date:** 15 November 2026  
**Service date:** 13 November 2026  
**Load:** 48291

| Description | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Road freight: Vienna → Rotterdam | €1,850 |
| Fuel surcharge | €200 |
| Detention | €120 |
| **Net total** | **€2,170** |

**VAT treatment:** As applicable to the transaction  
**Legal note:** Reverse charge, where applicable and correctly determined  
**Payment terms:** Net 30

The real invoice should contain the exact VAT treatment and wording required for the transaction.

## Common EU transport invoice mistakes

### Using the wrong legal customer

The invoice should identify the entity that actually purchased the service.

### Missing VAT IDs where required

Cross-border B2B transactions often depend heavily on correct VAT identification.

### Treating the CMR as the invoice

They are separate documents.

### Using “0% VAT” as a universal solution

Zero-rated, exempt, outside-scope and reverse-charge transactions are different.

### Forgetting the reverse-charge wording

When the customer is liable for the VAT, EU invoicing rules require the relevant wording.

### Assuming one EU country's rules cover all EU countries

EU rules are a baseline; national requirements still matter.

### Assuming a PDF satisfies every e-invoicing mandate

It may not.

## A practical pre-issue checklist

Before issuing a transport invoice, confirm:

- [ ] Correct legal supplier
- [ ] Correct legal customer
- [ ] Invoice number is unique
- [ ] Invoice date is correct
- [ ] Service date is included where required
- [ ] Supplier VAT ID is correct
- [ ] Customer VAT ID is included where required
- [ ] Transport service is described clearly
- [ ] Load/customer reference is correct
- [ ] Net amount is correct
- [ ] VAT treatment has been confirmed
- [ ] VAT rate/amount is correct where VAT is charged
- [ ] “Reverse charge” wording is present where required
- [ ] Total payable is clear
- [ ] Payment terms are clear
- [ ] Supporting CMR/POD documents are retained
- [ ] National e-invoicing requirements have been checked

## Frequently asked questions

### What information must an EU transport invoice contain?

EU VAT rules define a core set of invoice information including invoice identification, supplier/customer information, transaction details, VAT information and the total. Additional requirements can apply based on the transaction and Member State.

### Is a CMR consignment note the same as an invoice?

No. The CMR note documents the contract of carriage and shipment information. The invoice requests payment and fulfils commercial/VAT billing functions.

### Do all EU countries have identical invoice requirements?

No. EU VAT rules provide the common baseline, while Member States can have additional national requirements.

### Is a PDF invoice enough in every EU country?

No. Some Member States require or are introducing structured B2B e-invoicing systems. Businesses must check the requirements that apply in each market.

### Must a reverse-charge invoice say “reverse charge”?

Yes. EU VAT invoicing rules require those words when the customer is liable for the tax under reverse charge.

### Can a CMR be attached to the invoice?

Yes. It can be an important supporting transport document, depending on the shipment and customer workflow.

## One invoice, two layers of compliance

For an EU transport company, a strong invoice has two layers:

**EU VAT baseline**
→ Core invoice and VAT information

**Applicable national/transaction rules**
→ Local invoicing, e-invoicing, reporting and specific wording

Then the supporting transport record sits alongside it:

**CMR / POD / transport documents**

Keeping those roles separate makes cross-border billing much easier to understand.
