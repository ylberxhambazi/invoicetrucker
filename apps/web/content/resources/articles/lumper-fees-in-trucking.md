# Lumper Fees in Trucking: Who Pays and How to Add Them to an Invoice

A lumper fee is a charge for third-party labor used to load or unload freight, commonly at warehouses, distribution centers, and grocery facilities.

For carriers, the important part is not only paying the lumper. It is making sure the charge is documented correctly so it can be reimbursed when the agreement allows it.

A typical workflow looks like:

**Driver arrives → Facility requires lumper → Carrier/broker authorizes payment → Driver pays → Receipt collected → Fee added to invoice → Receipt attached**

If one step is missing, reimbursement can be delayed.

## What is a lumper fee?

A lumper fee is the amount charged by workers or a third-party service to load, unload, sort, restack, or handle freight.

The driver may be instructed to pay the fee at the facility using Comchek, EFS, a card, cash, a broker-issued payment code, or another approved method.

## Who pays lumper fees?

The driver or carrier may pay the lumper initially, but the final cost may be reimbursed by the broker, shipper, or customer depending on the load agreement.

Do not assume every lumper fee is reimbursable. Before paying, check the rate confirmation, broker instructions, customer policy, and required approval process.

## Should you call the broker before paying a lumper?

When possible, yes.

A good process is:

1. Facility tells the driver a lumper is required.
2. Driver or dispatcher contacts the broker.
3. Broker confirms whether the charge is approved.
4. Driver pays using the instructed method.
5. Driver obtains a receipt.
6. Receipt is attached to the billing package.

This creates a clear record before the invoice is submitted.

## What documentation should you keep?

At minimum, keep:

- Lumper receipt
- Load number
- Facility
- Date
- Amount
- Broker/customer approval when required

You may also keep a payment code, transaction confirmation, photo/PDF of the receipt, revised rate confirmation, or broker message.

The goal is simple:

**Show what was paid, why it was paid, and which load it belongs to.**

## How do you add a lumper fee to a trucking invoice?

Treat it as a separate accessorial line.

| Charge | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Line haul | $2,400 |
| Fuel surcharge | $300 |
| Lumper fee | $95 |
| **Total Due** | **$2,795** |

Do not hide the lumper amount inside line haul. Separate line items make review easier.

## Should the invoice amount match the rate confirmation?

Usually yes, unless approved accessorials changed the final amount.

Suppose the original rate confirmation shows **$2,700** and a $95 approved lumper fee is added. The final invoice becomes **$2,795**.

That difference should be supported by the receipt, approval, and a revised rate confirmation if the broker issues one.

## Do lumper fees require a revised rate confirmation?

Sometimes. Some brokers update the rate confirmation. Others approve the charge through email, a broker portal, dispatch system, message, or payment authorization.

Follow the customer’s process. The important part is keeping a reliable record of approval.

## What if the lumper receipt is lost?

Contact the facility or payment provider as quickly as possible. You may be able to recover a transaction receipt, payment confirmation, electronic receipt, or broker payment record.

Do not wait until the invoice is rejected.

## Can a broker reject a lumper charge?

A broker may dispute or reject reimbursement if:

- Approval was required but not obtained
- Receipt is missing
- Amount does not match
- Receipt belongs to another load
- Charge is not covered by the agreement

That is why documentation matters.

## Lumper fees vs detention

They are different accessorials.

**Lumper fee:** pays for freight-handling labor.

**Detention:** compensates the carrier for excessive waiting time.

A load can have both.

| Charge | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Line haul | $2,500 |
| Fuel surcharge | $300 |
| Lumper | $90 |
| Detention | $150 |
| **Total Due** | **$3,040** |

Each charge should have its own supporting documentation.

## Keep the receipt connected to the load

One of the easiest ways to lose reimbursement is document fragmentation.

The driver sends the lumper receipt through text. The dispatcher has the rate confirmation in email. The invoice is in Excel. Nobody remembers which image belongs to which load.

A better structure is:

**Load 48291**
- Rate confirmation
- BOL
- POD
- Lumper receipt
- Detention approval
- Invoice
- Payment status

That makes billing faster and reduces mistakes.

## A simple lumper reimbursement checklist

Before sending the invoice:

- [ ] Correct load number
- [ ] Correct lumper amount
- [ ] Receipt attached
- [ ] Receipt is readable
- [ ] Broker/customer approval retained
- [ ] Revised rate confirmation attached if applicable
- [ ] Lumper shown as separate invoice line
- [ ] Final invoice total checked

## Common lumper billing mistakes

### No receipt

This is the most obvious problem.

### Receipt image is unreadable

A blurry photo may be treated like no receipt at all.

### Wrong load

Receipts can look similar. Match the load number.

### No approval

If the broker required authorization, get it.

### Amount typed incorrectly

Always compare the invoice line against the actual receipt.

### Forgotten reimbursement

If the driver paid $120 but nobody adds it to the invoice, the carrier absorbs the cost.

## What should you do immediately after paying?

The driver should send the receipt into the company’s document workflow while still at the facility if possible.

A good habit is:

**Pay → photograph/scan → attach to load → notify dispatch**

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a lumper do?

A lumper is a worker or service that loads, unloads, sorts, restacks, or otherwise handles freight at a facility.

### Who reimburses the lumper fee?

It depends on the load agreement. The carrier may pay first and then invoice the broker or customer for reimbursement.

### Do I need a receipt?

Usually, yes. Many payers require proof of the amount before reimbursement.

### Should I add a lumper fee to line haul?

No. Itemize it separately.

### Is a lumper fee the same as detention?

No. Lumper fees pay for freight handling. Detention pays for excessive waiting time.

## Do not let small accessorials disappear

A $75, $100, or $150 fee may seem small compared with the freight rate, but repeated missed reimbursements add up quickly.

The simplest process is:

**Approve → Pay → Save receipt → Attach → Invoice → Track**
