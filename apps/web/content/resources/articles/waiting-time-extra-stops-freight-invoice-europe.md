# Billing Waiting Time and Extra Stops in European Road Freight

A truck that sits at a ramp for five hours or makes an extra delivery stop costs real money. If nobody wrote the charge down before the load, and nobody recorded the times, that money is usually lost.

This guide shows how a transport company can agree additional charges up front, record the evidence at the time, and put them on the invoice so they are paid without argument.

> **Note:** The wording and numbers below are examples to adapt, not legal advice. What you can charge depends on your agreement and the national law that applies to it.

## The charges we are talking about

Terms differ between countries and customers, so use the same words in your agreement and on your invoice:

- **Waiting time** (also called standing time): time beyond an agreed free period at loading or unloading
- **Extra stop**: an additional loading or unloading point not in the original order
- **Failed or repeated delivery**: the truck arrives and cannot unload, or has to return
- **Cancellation or unused truck**: the vehicle was ordered, but the load was cancelled or not ready

## Rule 1: Agree it before the load

If the agreement says nothing about waiting time, you may have no basis to charge for it. Put the terms in the rate agreement or transport order, even in a short form.

Sample wording to adapt:

```
Free loading/unloading time: [2] hours per stop, measured from arrival
confirmed by [gate check-in / written notice] until release of the
vehicle and documents.
Waiting time beyond free time: €[40] per [started hour / 30-minute block].
Additional stop: €[75] per stop, to be confirmed in writing before loading.
Cancellation with less than [x] hours' notice: €[amount].
Claims for additional charges must be reported within [x] days of delivery.
```

Points to settle:

- How the clock **starts** (arrival at the gate, check-in, or the booked time slot)
- How it **stops** (loading finished, or documents released)
- Whether the charge is per **started** hour or per **completed** block
- Whether extra stops need **written approval** before they happen
- The **deadline** for reporting and invoicing the charge

## Rule 2: Record the evidence when it happens

When a customer disputes a waiting-time charge, the fight is almost always about times. Keep records created at the time, not reconstructed later:

- A **written message** to the dispatcher or customer when the wait starts, with a timestamp
- **Gate or ramp check-in slips** showing arrival and departure times
- Times noted on the **transport documents** where this is allowed
- **Photos** of the queue or closed ramp, taken without capturing personal data
- **Driver and vehicle records**, such as telematics or tachograph timestamps, can support your times, subject to your own data protection rules

Also record your **arrival time against the appointed time**. If the truck was late, the customer will point it out.

## Rule 3: Itemise it on the invoice

Show each additional charge on its own line, with the load reference and the basis of the calculation. A line reading "Extras €160" is likely to be rejected.

### Worked example (illustrative)

Agreement: 2 hours free time, €40 per hour.
Arrival: 08:00. Loading finished and documents released: 13:30.

- Total time: 5 hours 30 minutes
- Free time: 2 hours
- Chargeable time: 3 hours 30 minutes

How the unit changes the result:

| Billing basis | Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per **started hour** | 4 hours × €40 | €160.00 |
| Per **30-minute block** (€20 each) | 7 blocks × €20 | €140.00 |

Both are valid, but only if the agreement says which one applies.

A complete set of invoice lines might look like this:

| Description | Qty | Unit price | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Road freight [origin] → [destination], load [reference] | 1 | €1,850.00 | €1,850.00 |
| Fuel surcharge, load [reference] | 1 | €104.06 | €104.06 |
| Waiting time at [place], [date]: arrival 08:00, release 13:30, 2 h free, 4 started hours | 4 | €40.00 | €160.00 |
| Additional stop at [place], confirmed by [name/date] | 1 | €75.00 | €75.00 |

The amounts are examples. For fuel surcharges, see [Fuel Surcharge for Road Freight in Europe: How to Calculate It](/resources/fuel-surcharge-road-freight-invoice-europe).

## Rule 4: Send it promptly, and consider a separate invoice

- **Invoice soon after delivery.** Memories fade and deadlines in the agreement may apply.
- **Attach or reference your evidence** when you send it.
- If a charge is likely to be disputed, a **separate invoice** for the additional charges lets the customer pay the undisputed freight on time.

## VAT on additional charges

Additional charges for the same transport are normally part of the price of the service, so they usually follow the same VAT treatment as the freight charge. VAT depends on the customer, the countries and the exact transaction, so confirm the treatment with your accountant.

## The most common disputes

| Customer says | What prevents it |
| --- | --- |
| "We never agreed to this" | Written terms before the load |
| "Your driver arrived late" | Recorded arrival time against the appointment |
| "The wait was not that long" | Timestamps from check-in and release |
| "We did not approve the extra stop" | Written approval before the stop |
| "We cannot reconcile this line" | Itemised lines with load reference and calculation |
| "This is too late to claim" | Invoicing within the agreed deadline |

## Checklist before you invoice

- Are waiting time and extra stops covered in the agreement or transport order?
- Do you have arrival and release times from a reliable record?
- Did you compare your arrival with the booked slot?
- Is each charge on its own line, with date, place and calculation?
- Does the line include the load or order reference?
- Is the invoice within the agreed deadline for claims?
- Is the additional charge supported by written approval where required?

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I always charge for waiting time?

No. It depends on your agreement and the law that applies to it. If the agreement is silent, do not assume you can charge. Agree the terms before the load.

### How much free time is normal?

There is no single figure. It varies between contracts and customers, so agree one explicitly.

### What if the customer's appointment was late, not my arrival?

Keep proof of your arrival time against the booked slot. Whether the wait is chargeable then depends on the terms you agreed.

### Should additional charges go on the freight invoice?

Usually as separate lines on the same invoice. If you expect a dispute, a separate invoice avoids delaying payment of the freight.

### Does a CMR note replace a waiting-time record?

No. A CMR note documents the contract of carriage. Keep separate time records for waiting time.
