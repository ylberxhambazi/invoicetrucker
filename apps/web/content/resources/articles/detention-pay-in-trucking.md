# Detention Pay in Trucking: How to Document and Invoice Detention Charges

Detention happens when a driver is kept waiting at a shipper or receiver longer than the agreed free time.

For an owner-operator or small fleet, that waiting time is not free. The truck is not moving, the driver may lose the next load, fuel may be burned while idling, and the entire schedule can be affected.

A good detention billing process looks like this:

**Arrive → Record time → Wait beyond free time → Document approval → Add detention to invoice → Attach supporting records**

The key is not simply adding a detention line to an invoice. The charge should be supported by the agreement and by reliable timing records.

## What is detention pay in trucking?

Detention pay is compensation for excessive waiting time at a shipper, receiver, warehouse, or other facility.

A load may allow a certain amount of free time before detention begins.

For example:

- Appointment: 8:00 AM
- Free time: 2 hours
- Driver released: 12:30 PM

The driver waited 4.5 hours total. If the first 2 hours are free, the potentially billable detention time is **2.5 hours**.

Whether all of that time is payable depends on the rate confirmation, broker agreement, customer rules, and how the delay was documented.

## Why detention matters to owner-operators

A delay can create costs beyond the waiting time itself:

- Missed pickup appointments
- Lost loads
- Reduced daily miles
- Extra idling fuel
- Driver-hours issues
- Rescheduling
- Additional parking or overnight costs

A carrier that never documents detention may be absorbing part of the customer’s operational delay.

## When does detention time start?

There is no universal rule that applies to every load. The agreement may define detention based on appointment time, actual arrival time, check-in time, dock time, or another event.

Example:

**Appointment:** 10:00 AM  
**Arrival:** 9:45 AM  
**Check-in:** 9:50 AM  
**Free time:** 2 hours  
**Release:** 1:15 PM

If the customer calculates detention from the appointment time, billable time may begin at noon. If it calculates from check-in, the result may be different.

Always use the rule in the applicable agreement.

## What should you document for detention?

Good detention documentation may include:

- Load number
- Facility name
- Appointment time
- Arrival time
- Check-in time
- Dock time
- Loading/unloading completion time
- Release time
- Driver notes
- Broker approval
- Rate confirmation language
- Revised rate confirmation
- ELD or location evidence when relevant

Do not rely only on memory. A simple timeline is much easier to defend later.

## Example detention timeline

| Event | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Appointment | 9:00 AM |
| Driver arrival | 8:50 AM |
| Check-in | 8:55 AM |
| Dock assigned | 10:30 AM |
| Loading complete | 12:40 PM |
| Driver released | 12:50 PM |

If the agreement allows 2 hours of free time from the 9:00 AM appointment, detention begins at 11:00 AM.

Release is at 12:50 PM, so potential billable detention is **1 hour 50 minutes**. How that gets rounded depends on the agreement.

## How is detention pay calculated?

Common approaches include:

- Hourly rate
- Half-hour increments
- Full-hour increments
- Flat amount after free time

Example:

- Free time: 2 hours
- Billable detention: 2.5 hours
- Rate: $50/hour

Calculation:

**2.5 × $50 = $125**

Your invoice could show:

| Charge | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Line haul | $2,400 |
| Fuel surcharge | $300 |
| Detention | $125 |
| **Total Due** | **$2,825** |

Do not assume a detention rate. Use the rate or rule in the agreement.

## Should detention appear as a separate invoice line?

Yes. Detention should normally be itemized separately from line haul and fuel surcharge.

Example:

**Detention — 2.5 hours @ $50/hour — $125**

That makes the invoice easier to review and avoids the appearance that the base freight rate changed without explanation.

## Do you need broker approval before billing detention?

Often, yes. The exact process depends on the broker or shipper.

Some require:

- Notification when free time is about to expire
- A check call
- Email approval
- Portal approval
- Revised rate confirmation

Others may define detention rules directly in the rate confirmation.

The safest process is to notify the broker while the delay is happening, not days later when the invoice is created.

## What should be attached to a detention invoice?

Depending on the agreement, attach:

- Original or revised rate confirmation
- Written detention approval
- Arrival/departure record
- POD/BOL
- Facility receipt
- Other timing evidence required by the payer

## Common detention billing mistakes

### Waiting until invoicing to mention the delay

If the broker was never informed, the charge may be disputed.

### No arrival or release time

Without timing evidence, it is difficult to show how detention was calculated.

### Billing before free time expires

Detention generally applies after the agreed free-time period.

### Using the wrong rate

Do not invent a detention rate based on another customer.

### Hiding detention inside line haul

Itemize it.

### Missing approval

If approval is required, keep it with the load paperwork.

## Detention vs layover

Detention and layover are related but different.

**Detention** usually refers to waiting at a facility beyond the allowed free time.

**Layover** generally involves a delay long enough that the driver or truck must remain until a later period or day before continuing.

The agreement should define how each is compensated.

## Build detention into the load workflow

A simple internal workflow can be:

**Load booked** → Review detention terms  
**Driver arrives** → Record arrival/check-in  
**Free time nearly used** → Notify broker  
**Detention begins** → Continue recording times  
**Driver released** → Record release  
**Billing** → Attach approval + calculate charge

This reduces the chance that detention is forgotten.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is detention pay in trucking?

It is compensation for waiting beyond the agreed free time at a shipper, receiver, or other facility.

### How much is detention pay?

Rates vary by agreement. Use the amount and billing method defined by the broker, shipper, contract, or rate confirmation.

### When should I notify the broker?

Ideally before or when the free-time period expires, according to the agreed process.

### Can I invoice detention without approval?

That depends on the agreement. Many billing workflows require documented approval or compliance with a specific notification procedure.

### Should detention be on the same invoice as the load?

Usually yes, as a separate itemized accessorial charge.

## Turn waiting time into documented billing

Detention is easiest to collect when the carrier can clearly show:

**when the driver arrived → when free time ended → when the driver was released → how the charge was calculated**

Good documentation turns a disputed extra charge into a verifiable billing item.
