# What Documents Should You Attach to a Trucking Invoice?

A trucking invoice can be completely correct and still get delayed if the supporting paperwork is incomplete.

For many broker and shipper billing workflows, the invoice is only one part of the billing package.

Common supporting documents include:

- Signed Proof of Delivery (POD)
- Bill of Lading (BOL)
- Rate confirmation
- Lumper receipts
- Detention approval
- Scale tickets
- Revised rate confirmation
- Other approved accessorial documentation

The exact requirements vary by customer and load.

The safest rule is:

> **Send the documents the payer needs to verify the load, delivery, and amount being billed.**

## Why do trucking invoices need supporting documents?

The payer needs to verify three things:

1. **What was agreed?**
2. **Was the freight delivered?**
3. **Does the amount billed match the agreement and approved extras?**

Different documents answer different questions.

A simple workflow looks like:

**Rate Confirmation → BOL → POD → Invoice → Payment**

If accessorials occur, supporting documents are added to that chain.

## The basic trucking invoice package

For a typical brokered load, the billing package may include:

- Invoice
- Signed POD or signed BOL
- Rate confirmation

If there are additional charges, it may also include:

- Lumper receipt
- Detention approval
- Layover approval
- Stop-off approval
- Scale ticket
- Revised rate confirmation

Some customers require fewer documents. Others require more.

Always follow the customer’s billing instructions.

## 1. Proof of Delivery (POD)

A POD confirms that the shipment reached its destination.

It may show:

- Delivery date
- Receiver signature
- Delivery location
- Shipment reference
- Exceptions or damage notes

For many brokers, the POD is one of the most important documents in the billing package because it supports the carrier’s claim that the transportation service was completed.

A missing or unreadable POD can delay payment.

For more detail:

[Proof of Delivery vs Bill of Lading: What's the Difference?](/resources/proof-of-delivery-vs-bill-of-lading)

## 2. Bill of Lading (BOL)

The Bill of Lading documents the shipment.

Depending on the shipment, it may include:

- Shipper
- Consignee
- Origin
- Destination
- Commodity description
- Quantity
- Weight
- Shipment references
- Special handling instructions

A signed BOL may also serve as delivery evidence in some workflows.

The BOL is not the same thing as the invoice.

The BOL documents the freight. The invoice requests payment.

See:

[Bill of Lading vs Invoice: What's the Difference?](/resources/bill-of-lading-vs-invoice)

## 3. Rate confirmation

For brokered freight, the rate confirmation documents the commercial terms between the broker and carrier.

It commonly includes:

- Broker
- Carrier
- Load number
- Origin and destination
- Pickup and delivery information
- Line-haul amount
- Fuel surcharge
- Accessorial terms
- Total agreed rate

The invoice should normally match the rate confirmation unless there are documented approved changes.

See:

[Rate Confirmation vs Invoice: What's the Difference?](/resources/rate-confirmation-vs-invoice)

## 4. Lumper receipt

A lumper fee may be charged when third-party labor loads or unloads freight.

If the carrier pays the lumper and expects reimbursement, the customer may require:

- The receipt
- The amount
- Load reference
- Approval according to the agreement

Example:

| Charge | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Line haul | $2,400 |
| Fuel surcharge | $300 |
| Lumper | $85 |
| **Total Due** | **$2,785** |

Without the receipt, the payer may reject the extra $85.

## 5. Detention approval

Detention charges compensate for excessive waiting time under the applicable agreement.

A detention billing record may need to show:

- Arrival time
- Departure time
- Free-time allowance
- Billable detention period
- Approved rate
- Broker or shipper approval

Do not rely on memory or a phone conversation when billing detention.

Keep written documentation.

## 6. Layover approval

A layover occurs when the driver is delayed long enough that the load cannot continue as originally planned.

If the contract allows a layover charge, retain:

- Written approval
- Revised rate confirmation if issued
- Message or portal confirmation
- Any required timing evidence

The goal is the same: **make the extra charge easy to verify.**

## 7. Stop-off or additional-stop approval

A load may require an extra pickup or delivery stop.

If that extra stop changes the agreed rate, keep documentation showing:

- The added location
- The approved charge
- Updated load instructions
- Revised rate confirmation if applicable

Then itemize the charge separately on the invoice.

## 8. Scale ticket

Scale tickets may be relevant when:

- Weight needs verification
- The customer requires weight documentation
- Reweigh charges are billed
- Weight disputes arise

If a scale-related fee is being invoiced, attach the supporting ticket when required.

## 9. Revised rate confirmation

If the original rate changes, the cleanest supporting document is often a revised rate confirmation.

For example:

Original rate: **$2,700**

Additional approved charges:

- Detention: $150
- Lumper: $75

Updated total: **$2,925**

A revised rate confirmation makes the final amount easier for accounting to approve.

## 10. Other customer-specific documents

Some customers may request:

- Purchase order
- Delivery receipt
- Pallet receipt
- Temperature record
- Seal record
- Weight certificate
- Customs paperwork
- Fuel receipt
- Accessorial approval form
- Customer-specific billing cover sheet

These are not universal requirements.

Follow the actual contract and billing instructions.

## Which documents are usually mandatory?

There is no single universal package for every trucking invoice.

| Document | Common Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Invoice | Requests payment |
| Rate confirmation | Shows agreed carrier compensation |
| BOL | Documents the freight |
| POD | Confirms delivery |
| Lumper receipt | Supports lumper reimbursement |
| Detention approval | Supports detention charge |
| Scale ticket | Supports weight/scale-related billing |
| Revised ratecon | Supports changed commercial terms |

The payer may require only some of these.

## Should you attach both the BOL and POD?

Sometimes yes.

Sometimes a signed BOL effectively serves as the POD. Sometimes the customer expects separate documents.

Do not assume one customer’s process applies to every customer.

Check the billing instructions.

## Should you attach the rate confirmation to every invoice?

Not always.

Some brokers already have the rate confirmation in their system. Others want it included in the billing package.

If the customer requires it, attach it.

If not, keep it in your internal load record anyway. It is valuable for checking that the invoice amount is correct.

## What if the invoice total does not match the rate confirmation?

Do not send an unexplained mismatch.

If the invoice is higher, show why.

| Charge | Rate Confirmation | Final Invoice |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Line haul | $2,400 | $2,400 |
| Fuel surcharge | $300 | $300 |
| Detention | — | $150 |
| Lumper | — | $75 |
| **Total** | **$2,700** | **$2,925** |

Then attach the documents that support the extra $225.

## What makes supporting paperwork “good”?

A good document is:

- Readable
- Complete
- Matched to the correct load
- Properly signed where required
- Not cropped
- Not blurry
- Not missing key pages
- Easy to identify

A document that technically exists but cannot be read may still delay payment.

## File naming matters

If you store paperwork digitally, use consistent names.

Instead of:

`IMG_3498.jpg`

use:

`Load-48291-POD.pdf`

or:

`Load-48291-Lumper-Receipt.pdf`

This makes it easier to find and send the right file later.

## Keep documents connected to the load

A common small-fleet problem is fragmentation.

The rate confirmation is in email.

The POD is in the driver’s phone.

The invoice is in Excel.

The lumper receipt is in a messaging app.

The payment status is in a notebook.

That works until volume increases.

A better structure is:

**Load**
- Rate confirmation
- BOL
- POD
- Accessorials
- Invoice
- Payment status

Everything belongs to the same load record.

## What happens if a required document is missing?

Possible outcomes include:

- Invoice rejected
- Invoice placed on hold
- Payment delayed
- Accessorial denied
- Billing team requests resubmission
- Due-date clock starts later

The exact result depends on the payer.

The best solution is to catch missing paperwork before the invoice is sent.

## Pre-send document checklist

Before submitting a trucking invoice, check:

- [ ] Invoice is complete
- [ ] Load number matches
- [ ] Rate matches the agreement
- [ ] POD/BOL is attached if required
- [ ] Delivery signature is readable
- [ ] Rate confirmation is included if required
- [ ] Lumper receipt is attached
- [ ] Detention approval is attached
- [ ] Other accessorial approvals are attached
- [ ] File names are clear
- [ ] Documents belong to the correct load
- [ ] Invoice is sent to the correct billing destination

## Common paperwork mistakes

### Wrong POD attached

Easy to do when multiple loads are handled at once.

### Missing pages

Multi-page BOLs or receipts may be incomplete.

### Blurry phone photo

A readable document matters more than simply having a file.

### Accessorial without proof

The invoice includes a charge but nothing supports it.

### Old rate confirmation

A revised rate exists, but the original is attached.

### Wrong load number

The documents belong to one load while the invoice references another.

## How long should trucking invoice documents be kept?

There is no single retention period that applies to every document, company, customer, tax rule, or jurisdiction.

Retention can depend on:

- Contract requirements
- Tax obligations
- Accounting requirements
- Claims
- Transportation rules
- Customer requirements
- Factoring requirements

Keep the records your business needs to support billing, accounting, disputes, and applicable legal obligations.

## Digital documents vs paper

Digital documents are easier to:

- Search
- Attach
- Share
- Back up
- Match to invoices

But digitization only helps if the files are organized.

A folder full of unnamed PDFs can be almost as difficult as paper.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a POD to send a trucking invoice?

Many brokers require delivery documentation before processing payment, but the exact requirement depends on the customer.

### Is a signed BOL enough as proof of delivery?

Sometimes. A signed BOL may serve as POD in some workflows, while other customers require a separate delivery document.

### Should I send the rate confirmation with the invoice?

Send it when the customer’s billing process requires it. Keep it internally even when it is not required as an attachment.

### What documents support detention?

Common support includes arrival/departure times, written approval, and any revised rate documentation required by the agreement.

### What should I attach for a lumper fee?

Usually the lumper receipt and any approval required by the broker or shipper.

It shows:

**What was agreed → What was delivered → What is being billed → Why the total is correct**

That is what good trucking paperwork does.
