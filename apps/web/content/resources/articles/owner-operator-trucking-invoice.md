# Owner-Operator Trucking Invoice: How to Bill Brokers and Shippers

For an owner-operator, getting the load delivered is only part of the job.

You still need to turn that completed load into a clean invoice, send the right paperwork, and track the payment until the money actually arrives.

A simple owner-operator billing workflow looks like this:

**Rate Confirmation → Load Completed → POD/BOL → Invoice → Send → Track → Paid**

The invoice itself does not need to be complicated. It needs to be accurate, easy to match to the load, and supported by the documents your broker or shipper requires.

## What should an owner-operator trucking invoice include?

A practical owner-operator invoice should usually include:

- Your legal business or carrier name
- Business address and contact information
- MC and USDOT numbers when applicable
- Unique invoice number
- Invoice date
- Broker or shipper information
- Load or shipment number
- Pickup and delivery locations
- Pickup and delivery dates
- Line-haul charge
- Fuel surcharge
- Accessorial charges
- Total amount due
- Payment terms
- Remittance instructions
- Supporting-document references

## Billing a broker vs billing a direct shipper

The basic invoice structure is similar, but the workflow can be different.

### When billing a broker

The broker typically already has a load number and rate confirmation.

Your invoice should make it easy for the broker to match the bill to that load.

Pay close attention to:

- Broker load number
- Rate confirmation amount
- POD/BOL requirements
- Accessorial approvals
- Billing email or portal
- QuickPay or standard payment terms

A typical broker billing package may contain:

**Invoice + signed POD/BOL + rate confirmation + accessorial receipts**

### When billing a direct shipper

A direct shipper may use:

- Purchase-order numbers
- Customer account numbers
- Shipment references
- Contract rates
- Different billing contacts

Confirm the customer's required references before sending the invoice.

The rule is the same in both cases:

**Make the invoice easy for someone in accounts payable to match without asking you questions.**

## Start with the rate confirmation

For brokered freight, the rate confirmation is one of the most important documents in the billing process.

DAT describes a rate confirmation, or ratecon, as the agreement of pay between the freight broker and carrier. It documents the commercial terms the carrier accepted for the load.

Before creating the invoice, compare:

- Load number
- Origin
- Destination
- Pickup and delivery details
- Line-haul amount
- Fuel surcharge
- Additional stops
- Accessorial terms
- Payment instructions

Your invoice should match the agreed rate unless there is documented justification for additional charges.

## Collect the POD before billing when required

After delivery, get the signed delivery paperwork as quickly as possible.

Depending on the customer, this may be:

- Signed Proof of Delivery
- Signed Bill of Lading
- Electronic POD
- Customer-specific delivery receipt

Many broker workflows use signed POD or BOL documentation to confirm that the service was completed before paying the carrier.

## Match the invoice to the load

Before sending the invoice, compare these fields:

| Check | Rate Confirmation / Load | Invoice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Load number | 48291 | 48291 |
| Origin | Chicago, IL | Chicago, IL |
| Destination | Nashville, TN | Nashville, TN |
| Line haul | $2,400 | $2,400 |
| Fuel surcharge | $300 | $300 |
| Additional charges | Approved as applicable | Itemized |

A short check can prevent days of back-and-forth.

## How should owner-operators bill accessorial charges?

Accessorial charges should be itemized and supported by documentation.

Common examples include:

- Detention
- Lumper fees
- Layover
- Stop-off charges
- Driver assist
- Reconsignment
- Truck ordered not used

Suppose the rate confirmation is:

**Line haul: $2,400**  
**Fuel surcharge: $300**

Then a $75 lumper charge is added.

| Charge | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Line haul | $2,400.00 |
| Fuel surcharge | $300.00 |
| Lumper fee | $75.00 |
| **Total Due** | **$2,775.00** |

Attach the receipt if required.

For detention or another negotiated accessorial, retain the approval or revised rate confirmation where applicable.

## Payment terms: Net 30, QuickPay, and factoring

Your payment method may be:

- Net 15
- Net 30
- Net 45
- Broker QuickPay
- Factoring

### Standard payment terms

If the broker agreement says Net 30, your invoice should reflect that arrangement.

Putting "Net 15" on the invoice does not automatically override the contract.

### QuickPay

Some brokers offer faster payment in exchange for a fee.

The billing workflow may require choosing QuickPay through the broker's portal or following specific instructions.

### Factoring

Factoring allows a carrier to sell or assign approved invoices to a factoring provider in exchange for faster access to cash.

If you factor invoices, follow the factor's requirements carefully.

The remittance section may need to direct the broker or shipper to pay the factoring company rather than your trucking business.

## What documents should an owner-operator send with the invoice?

There is no universal billing package for every customer.

Common documents include:

- Invoice
- Signed POD or signed BOL
- Rate confirmation
- Lumper receipt
- Detention approval
- Scale ticket
- Revised rate confirmation

Follow the actual billing instructions for the customer.

## Should you invoice immediately after delivery?

Usually, yes — once the required paperwork is complete.

If the load delivers Monday and you wait until Friday to send the invoice, you have added four days to your own payment cycle.

A better process is:

**Delivered → POD received → invoice checked → invoice sent**

The goal is not to rush an incomplete invoice. The goal is to remove unnecessary delay.

## Common owner-operator invoicing mistakes

### Waiting several days after delivery

The payment clock cannot start before the invoice reaches the payer.

### Sending the invoice to the dispatcher instead of billing

Use the customer's billing email or portal.

### Wrong load number

The amount can be correct and still be delayed if accounts payable cannot match it to the shipment.

### Missing POD

A broker may hold the invoice until signed delivery documentation is provided.

### Rate confirmation mismatch

If the invoice amount differs from the confirmed rate, explain and document the difference.

### Unsupported accessorials

Do not add detention, lumper, or other charges without the documentation required by the agreement.

### Duplicate invoices

Keep unique invoice numbers and track what has already been sent.

### Tracking payment only in your inbox

An email marked "sent" does not mean the invoice is paid.

## A simple invoice-status system

Even a one-truck business should know the status of every invoice.

A useful workflow is:

- Draft
- Ready to send
- Sent
- Due
- Overdue
- Paid

This answers the more important question:

**Which loads have not turned into cash yet?**

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an owner-operator invoice a broker directly?

Yes. In a typical broker-carrier relationship, the carrier invoices the broker according to the broker's billing instructions and the agreed rate confirmation.

### Does the invoice need the MC or USDOT number?

Many carriers include them because they help identify the business. Whether they are specifically required depends on the transaction, applicable rules, and customer requirements.

### Do I need a signed POD?

Many brokers require signed delivery documentation before processing payment. Follow the billing instructions for the specific load.

### Should the invoice amount match the rate confirmation?

Normally yes, except for legitimate additional charges that were properly authorized and documented.

### What if I use a factoring company?

Follow the factoring company's document and remittance requirements. The broker may need to send payment directly to the factor.

### How soon should I invoice after delivery?

As soon as practical after the load is complete and the required supporting paperwork is available.
